# 01. The idea

Most of us keep our lives in twenty apps. Notes in one, tasks in another, email somewhere, files on a drive, people in our heads. An AI assistant can only help with what it can see, so it sees almost nothing.

The one-folder idea flips that. Everything goes in one folder on your computer, synced by Dropbox. Plain text files (markdown). The AI opens that folder and reads it. Now it knows your courses, your people, your projects, your rules.

## Four moves (ICOR)

- Input: things arrive. Voice memos, photos, PDFs, emails you drop in. They land in `team-inbox/`.
- Control: the AI sorts them. To-dos go to `todo.md`, people get a file in `people/`, ideas go to `topics/`.
- Output: the AI makes things. Handouts, slides, emails (as drafts), plans. They land in `owners-inbox/_New/` for you to review.
- Refine: you correct it. Every correction becomes a rule in a file, so the mistake does not come back.

## Why plain files

- You own them. No company can lock you out.
- Any AI can read them. Switch tools any time.
- Obsidian turns the same folder into a clickable map of your life.
- Dropbox gives you history and a second machine for free.

## The real secret

The folder is not the product. The rules are. After a year, the system knows hundreds of small preferences ("no bold text in handouts", "always put the frost date next to a sowing date") because each one was said once and written down. That is the Refine step, and it is where the value builds up.
